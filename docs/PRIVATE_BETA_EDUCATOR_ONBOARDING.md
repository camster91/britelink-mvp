# BriteLink Private Beta Educator Onboarding

**Welcome to the BriteLink educator team.** This guide will prepare you to deliver high-quality, personalized homeschool curriculum plans through the BriteLink platform.

## Your Role

As a BriteLink educator, you are the human expertise that differentiates us from AI tutoring apps. You:

- Review intake forms and clarify family learning contexts
- Design personalized 8-week curriculum plans
- Independently review colleague plans before publication
- Respond to parent messages with professional guidance
- Revise plans when family needs change
- Maintain quality, safeguarding, and accessibility standards

**You are not:**
- A chatbot or AI assistant (you're a credentialed human educator)
- A tutor providing direct instruction to children (you guide parents)
- Available 24/7 (you work defined business hours with SLA commitments)

## Service Model

### Parent-Guided Learning

BriteLink plans are **designed for parents to execute with their children**, not for children to follow independently.

**This means:**
- Instructions target the parent/caregiver, not the child
- You estimate "adult help required" for each lesson
- You provide adaptations for different learning styles
- You include materials the family can access at home

**You are designing:**
- ✅ "Read the story aloud together. Pause after each page to ask your child to predict what happens next."
- ❌ "Read this story and answer the questions at the end."

### Quality Over Speed

**Our competitive advantage is human accountability and review.** Every plan must meet these standards:

1. **Personalized to intake:** Reflects the child's age, objectives, learning style, available time, and resources
2. **Independently reviewed:** Author ≠ reviewer. Fresh eyes catch errors and quality issues.
3. **Safeguarded:** Age-appropriate, no harmful content, educator-verified resources only
4. **Accessible:** Clear instructions, adaptations for different needs, reasonable time estimates
5. **Executable:** Parents can follow the plan without constant educator support

**SLA commitment:**
- New plans: 5-7 business days from usable intake
- Revisions: 5-7 business days from request
- Messages: 1 business day response target

**During beta:** These timelines may stretch as we refine workflows. Communicate honestly with families about delays.

**6. Delivery and Revision**
- Publish approved plan to family
- Retry failed deliveries
- Handle revision requests

## Core Workflows

### Workflow 1: New Case Intake Review

**When:** A new family submits intake and purchases a package.

**Your job:** Verify intake is usable for curriculum design.

**Steps:**

1. **Select case from queue**
   - Queue shows "Usable intake check required"
   - SLA clock starts at payment, not intake submission

2. **Review intake for completeness**
   - Age and grade level provided?
   - Learning objectives specific and achievable?
   - Subjects clearly identified?
   - Available time realistic?
   - Learning style or special needs mentioned?

3. **Decide: Usable or clarification needed**
   
   **Usable intake criteria:**
   - Enough detail to design a personalized plan
   - Objectives align with age/grade
   - Time commitment is clear
   - No critical missing information
   
   **Clarification needed if:**
   - Objectives are too vague ("learn stuff" vs "master multiplication")
   - Age/grade mismatch is unclear
   - Time availability is ambiguous
   - Special accommodations mentioned but not detailed

4. **If clarification needed, send message**
   - Be specific: "To design the best plan for [child], I need to understand..."
   - Ask 1-3 focused questions
   - Give examples if helpful
   - Set expectation: "I'll start designing once I hear back"

5. **If usable, mark ready for assignment**
   - Queue updates to show "Ready for educator assignment"
   - Admin assigns case to you or another educator
   - SLA clock continues

**Tips:**
- Don't over-clarify. If you can design a good plan, it's usable.
- Most families provide enough detail in initial intake.
- Clarification messages should feel supportive, not interrogative.

### Workflow 2: Plan Authoring

**When:** A case is assigned to you as author.

**Your job:** Design a personalized 8-week curriculum that meets the family's objectives.

**Steps:**

1. **Study the intake context**
   - Read every field carefully
   - Note learning objectives, time availability, resources, learning style
   - Identify any accommodations or special considerations

2. **Plan the 8-week arc**
   - What's the progression of skills or concepts?
   - How do weeks build on each other?
   - Are objectives achievable in 8 weeks?
   - Which subjects per week?

3. **Design week 1 in detail**
   - 5 days of lessons (Monday-Friday default, adjust if family specifies otherwise)
   - Each lesson: objective, instructions, materials, adaptations, adult-help estimate
   - Select governed resources (or request substitutes if needed)
   - Verify total weekly time matches family's availability

4. **Draft remaining weeks**
   - Week-by-week progression
   - Adjust difficulty and pace based on age/objectives
   - Include variety (reading, hands-on, creative, assessment)
   - Plan for review and practice, not just new concepts

5. **Quality self-check**
   - Is this plan personalized to this child's intake?
   - Are instructions clear for a parent to follow?
   - Are materials accessible (not requiring expensive purchases)?
   - Are resources educator-verified and age-appropriate?
   - Are adaptations provided for different learning needs?
   - Is adult-help time realistic?

6. **Save and publish for review**
   - Save draft frequently (not auto-saved)
   - When complete, publish for review
   - Plan moves to review queue
   - You cannot edit once published (reviewer must send back for changes)

**Tips:**
- Start with week 1 in full detail—it sets the tone
- Copy/adapt lesson structures across weeks for efficiency
- Don't over-design. Parents need clear, simple instructions.
- Include enough detail that a parent can execute without messaging you.
- Estimate adult help honestly. 30 min/lesson is realistic; 2 hours/lesson is not.

### Workflow 3: Plan Review

**When:** A colleague publishes a plan for review.

**Your job:** Independently verify quality before the family sees it.

**Steps:**

1. **Select case from review queue**
   - Read intake context first (don't just review the plan blind)
   - Note author name (you're checking their work)

2. **Review for personalization**
   - Does the plan reflect the child's age and grade?
   - Do lessons address the stated learning objectives?
   - Is the learning style preference incorporated?
   - Are time estimates matched to family availability?

3. **Review for safety and appropriateness**
   - Are resources age-appropriate?
   - Are links educator-verified (not random internet results)?
   - Is content free of harmful, biased, or inappropriate material?
   - Are activities safe for home execution (no dangerous materials)?

4. **Review for accessibility**
   - Are instructions clear and specific?
   - Are adaptations provided for different learning needs?
   - Can a parent follow this without constant support?
   - Are materials accessible (not requiring specialty items)?

5. **Review for executability**
   - Can you imagine a parent doing this lesson?
   - Are materials listed completely?
   - Are instructions in logical order?
   - Are adult-help estimates realistic?

6. **Decide: Approve or request changes**
   
   **Approve if:**
   - Plan meets all quality criteria
   - Minor imperfections are acceptable (don't nitpick)
   - You'd be comfortable if this were delivered to your own family
   
   **Request changes if:**
   - Plan doesn't match intake (wrong age, missing objectives, wrong time)
   - Safety or appropriateness concerns
   - Instructions are unclear or incomplete
   - Resources are broken or inappropriate
   - Executability is questionable

7. **If requesting changes, provide clear feedback**
   - Be specific: "Week 2, Day 3: This lesson requires materials not listed in the intake (telescope)"
   - Be constructive: "Consider adapting this to use household items instead"
   - Focus on fixable issues, not style preferences

8. **If approving, publish to family**
   - Plan moves to delivery queue
   - Family receives notification
   - Author and reviewer are both credited in audit log

**Tips:**
- You are the last line of defense before families see the plan. Take this seriously.
- Don't approve plans just to hit SLA. Quality matters more.
- If you're unsure, ask a senior educator or Cameron.
- Document your review checklist decisions in the workbench.

### Workflow 4: Secure Messaging

**When:** A family sends a message or you need to follow up.

**Your job:** Provide professional, supportive guidance within 1 business day.

**Steps:**

1. **Read the message in case context**
   - What are they asking?
   - Is this about a specific lesson? (Check week/day/lesson)
   - Have they tried the suggested approach?
   - Is this a clarification, a problem, or feedback?

2. **Respond professionally and supportively**
   
   **Good response structure:**
   - Acknowledge their question/concern
   - Provide specific guidance or answer
   - Offer next steps or alternatives
   - Invite follow-up if needed
   
   **Tone:**
   - Warm but professional
   - Non-judgmental (families are doing their best)
   - Specific and actionable
   - Empowering (you're coaching them, not taking over)

3. **Example responses**
   
   **Question:** "Week 2, Day 3: My child can't sound out the vocabulary words. What should I do?"
   
   **Response:** "Thanks for reaching out. It sounds like Week 2, Day 3 might be introducing vocabulary that's a bit advanced right now. Here are a few adaptations you can try:
   
   1. Pre-teach the words the day before using picture cards
   2. Break words into smaller chunks (syllables) and practice each part
   3. Use the words in context (conversation, story) before asking your child to read them
   
   If these adaptations help, continue with the week as planned. If your child is still struggling after trying these approaches, let me know and we can discuss a revision to adjust the reading level.
   
   You're doing great supporting their learning!"
   
   **Question:** "This is taking way longer than the estimate. Is that normal?"
   
   **Response:** "Thanks for the feedback. Adult-help estimates are averages—some families move faster, some slower, especially in the first few weeks as you're both getting used to the routine.
   
   A few questions to help me understand:
   - Which lessons are taking longer? (specific week/day if possible)
   - What part takes the most time? (gathering materials, explaining, practice, etc.)
   - Is your child engaged, or are they resisting?
   
   Once I understand the pattern, I can suggest adjustments or we can plan a revision if the pace needs to change overall."

4. **Escalate if needed**
   - Safety concerns → Cameron immediately
   - Service issues (can't access plan, data missing) → Cameron
   - Revision request → Follow revision workflow
   - Refund request → Cameron (educators don't handle payment disputes)

**Tips:**
- Respond within 1 business day (beta target)
- Be specific—vague advice doesn't help
- Empower parents to solve problems (they're the expert on their child)
- Document patterns (if multiple families ask the same question, improve the plan template)

### Workflow 5: Revision Requests

**When:** A family formally requests a revision (not just a message).

**Your job:** Design an updated plan addressing their concerns.

**Steps:**

1. **Review the revision request**
   - What specifically isn't working?
   - Have they tried the plan for at least 1-2 weeks?
   - What's their desired outcome?

2. **Review activity data**
   - Which lessons were completed, skipped, or rescheduled?
   - Are there patterns? (all math lessons skipped, all reading completed)
   - Do caregiver notes provide insight?

3. **Review prior messages**
   - Did they ask questions before requesting revision?
   - Did you suggest adaptations they tried?
   - Is this a persistent issue or a one-time problem?

4. **Decide: Revision or coaching**
   
   **Revision is appropriate if:**
   - Plan difficulty is mismatched (too easy or too hard consistently)
   - Time commitment is unrealistic (not just one busy week)
   - Learning style mismatch is causing frustration
   - Missing subjects or objectives from intake
   - Resources are inaccessible
   
   **Coaching is better if:**
   - They haven't tried the plan for 1-2 weeks
   - Issue is solvable with adaptations (already provided)
   - Problem is external (child was sick, family emergency)
   - Expectations need adjustment (not every lesson will go perfectly)

5. **If coaching, message them**
   - Acknowledge their frustration
   - Explain why you're suggesting coaching instead of revision
   - Provide specific guidance
   - Offer to revisit in 1-2 weeks if still not working

6. **If revising, design the updated plan**
   - Keep what's working (don't redesign from scratch)
   - Address the specific concerns
   - Adjust difficulty, pace, or focus as needed
   - Publish for independent review (same process as new plans)

7. **Deliver revision with explanation**
   - Brief message explaining what you changed and why
   - Set expectations (this revision addresses X, but may need further adjustment)
   - Invite feedback after 1-2 weeks

**Tips:**
- Revisions are entitled but finite (1 per Complete package, 4 per Annual)
- Use revisions wisely—coach when possible, revise when necessary
- Families aren't always right about what needs to change (you're the expert)
- Document revision patterns to improve initial plan quality

### Workflow 6: Delivery and Exceptions

**When:** Plan is approved and ready for family, or something goes wrong.

**Your job:** Ensure families receive their plans and handle service issues professionally.

**Steps:**

1. **Standard delivery**
   - Approved plan is automatically delivered
   - Family receives notification
   - Acknowledgment requested (family confirms receipt)
   - No educator action needed unless delivery fails

2. **Failed delivery (rare)**
   - Retry once automatically
   - If second failure, escalate to Cameron
   - Do not message family until issue is resolved

3. **Educator absence**
   - If you'll be unavailable >2 days, notify Cameron immediately
   - Cases will be reassigned
   - SLA will be adjusted
   - Family will be notified (not by you—by admin)

4. **Service exceptions**
   - Refund/chargeback → Case is placed on hold, notify Cameron
   - Privacy request → Follow privacy operations runbook
   - Safeguarding concern → Escalate to Cameron immediately
   - Technical issue → Report to Cameron, message family with workaround if possible

**Tips:**
- Don't promise delivery dates—SLA is 5-7 business days
- Don't handle payment disputes—that's Cameron's role
- Don't diagnose technical issues—report them and let engineering fix
- Document exceptions in case notes for future reference

## Quality Standards

### Personalization Checklist

Every plan must be personalized to the intake. Check:

- [ ] Age and grade level match lesson difficulty
- [ ] Learning objectives from intake are addressed
- [ ] Subjects requested are included (and not extras they didn't ask for)
- [ ] Learning style preferences are incorporated
- [ ] Available time per day matches lesson length estimates
- [ ] Resources match family's stated access (internet, books, materials)
- [ ] Accommodations or special needs mentioned in intake are supported

**Red flags:**
- Generic lessons that could apply to any child
- Ignoring stated learning objectives
- Requiring resources family doesn't have
- Time estimates exceed family's availability

### Safety and Safeguarding Checklist

Every plan must be safe and age-appropriate. Check:

- [ ] No harmful, violent, or inappropriate content
- [ ] Resources are educator-verified (not random internet results)
- [ ] Activities are safe for home execution (no dangerous materials or experiments)
- [ ] Content is unbiased and inclusive
- [ ] No external communication with child (links go to content, not chat rooms)
- [ ] No requests for child's personal information
- [ ] Privacy-respecting (doesn't ask family to post publicly)

**Red flags:**
- Unvetted external links
- Science experiments requiring chemicals or heat
- Content depicting violence, bias, or adult themes
- Activities requiring child to interact with strangers online

### Accessibility Checklist

Every plan must be executable by diverse families. Check:

- [ ] Instructions are clear and specific (not vague or assuming background knowledge)
- [ ] Materials are listed completely (not "gather materials" without listing them)
- [ ] Adaptations provided for different learning needs (visual, auditory, kinesthetic)
- [ ] Adult-help estimates are realistic (30-60 min/lesson typical)
- [ ] Resources are accessible (free or low-cost, not requiring specialty subscriptions)
- [ ] Instructions are step-by-step and sequential

**Red flags:**
- "Figure it out" or "explore freely" without scaffolding
- Requiring expensive materials or subscriptions
- No adaptations for different learning styles
- Assuming parent has teaching background

### Executability Checklist

Every plan must be doable by a parent at home. Check:

- [ ] You can imagine yourself doing this lesson with a child
- [ ] Steps are in logical order
- [ ] Success criteria are clear (how do you know the lesson is complete?)
- [ ] Materials are household items or easily obtained
- [ ] Instructions don't require constant educator support

**Red flags:**
- "Contact me for next steps" (plan should be self-contained)
- Vague instructions ("practice this skill")
- No clear endpoint (how do you know when to move on?)
- Requiring real-time educator assistance

## Messaging Best Practices

### Tone and Voice

**BriteLink tone:** Calm, professional, supportive, trustworthy (like Linear or Stripe, not chatty or salesy)

**Do:**
- Use clear, concise language
- Be specific and actionable
- Acknowledge parent effort and challenges
- Offer solutions, not just empathy
- Empower parents to solve problems

**Don't:**
- Use educational jargon (IEP, scaffolding, formative assessment) without explaining
- Be overly casual ("Hey! That sounds tough!")
- Be condescending ("You should already know this")
- Offer generic advice ("Just keep trying!")

### Example Messages

**Clarification request:**

"Thank you for your intake! To design the best plan for [child], I need a bit more detail about their math background. You mentioned they're working on multiplication—are they:

1. Just starting to learn the concept?
2. Practicing memorizing facts (1-12)?
3. Applying multiplication to word problems?

This will help me set the right starting point for Week 1. I'll begin designing as soon as I hear back!"

**Response to question:**

"Great question about Week 3, Day 2. The estimation activity is meant to build number sense before jumping into exact calculations. If your child is struggling with the concept, try these adaptations:

1. Use physical objects (blocks, toys) to make groups and estimate quantities
2. Start with smaller numbers (10-50 instead of 100-500)
3. Practice with everyday items (How many steps to the car? How many crackers in the box?)

The goal is for them to get comfortable with 'about how many' before worrying about exact answers. Let me know if these help!"

**Revision guidance:**

"Thanks for sharing that the reading level is too advanced. Looking at your activity data, I see Week 1-2 reading lessons took 2x the estimated time and several were rescheduled.

I recommend a revision to adjust the reading level and provide more phonics scaffolding. This will mean:
- Shorter passages with simpler vocabulary
- Pre-teaching key words before each reading
- More picture support and context clues

I'll design the revision this week and have it ready for review by [date]. In the meantime, you can continue with the math and science lessons—those seem to be going well!"

## Capacity and Caseload

### Target Caseload

**Sustainable per educator:**
- 8-12 active cases (authoring, revising, messaging)
- 4-6 cases awaiting family acknowledgment (monitoring only)
- 2-4 reviews per week (independent quality checks)

**During beta:** Caseload may be lower (5-10 families total across 2-3 educators)

### Managing Capacity

**When you're at capacity:**
1. Notify Cameron before accepting new assignments
2. Prioritize SLA-critical cases (oldest first)
3. Batch similar work (author all week 1 plans, then all week 2, etc.)
4. Use messaging to coach instead of immediately revising

**If you're consistently over capacity:**
- Work with Cameron to hire additional educators
- Identify process improvements (template lessons, resource library)
- Raise SLA targets to sustainable levels

**Burnout prevention:**
- Take breaks between cases
- Don't work outside business hours
- Ask for help before you're overwhelmed
- Celebrate wins (family feedback, completed packages)

## Appendix: Compliance and Ethics

### Privacy and Confidentiality

**You have access to sensitive family data:**
- Child's age, learning needs, household context
- Messages may include personal information
- Activity data shows daily family routines

**You must:**
- Only access cases assigned to you
- Never discuss cases outside secure channels
- Never share family information with other families or publicly
- Report privacy violations immediately

**You must not:**
- Access cases out of curiosity
- Share stories about families (even anonymized) on social media
- Discuss cases with colleagues in public spaces
- Store family data outside BriteLink platform

### Mandatory Reporting

**If you are a mandatory reporter:** Follow your jurisdiction's mandatory reporting obligations for suspected child abuse or neglect.

**If you suspect harm:** Report to Cameron and follow legal obligations. Do not conduct your own investigation.

### Bias and Inclusion

**BriteLink serves diverse families:**
- Different races, ethnicities, religions, and cultures
- Different abilities and learning needs
- Different family structures
- Different socioeconomic backgrounds

**You must:**
- Design plans that respect all backgrounds
- Avoid biased or stereotypical content
- Provide culturally responsive adaptations
- Never make assumptions about family values or beliefs

**You must not:**
- Impose your personal beliefs on families
- Judge family choices (curriculum preferences, learning pace, etc.)
- Recommend resources that are biased or non-inclusive
- Assume all families have the same resources or priorities

### Professional Boundaries

**You are a professional service provider, not a friend or therapist.**

**Maintain boundaries:**
- Use professional language and tone
- Don't share personal information about yourself
- Don't form personal relationships with families
- Don't communicate outside BriteLink platform
- Don't continue relationships after service ends

**If a family crosses boundaries:**
- Politely redirect to appropriate channel
- Document the interaction
- Escalate to Cameron if concerning
